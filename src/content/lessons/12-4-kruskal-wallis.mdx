The linear model compares group **means**. When the scores in a group are skewed,
or a few extreme scores drag a mean away from where most people are, you can
compare the groups on **ranks** instead. With two groups that is the Mann-Whitney
test (`wilcox.test()`); with three or more it is the Kruskal-Wallis test.

<CodeBlock id="c-load" code={`library(dplyr)

d <- read.csv("data/workplace.csv", stringsAsFactors = TRUE)

d %>%
  group_by(department) %>%
  summarise(n = n(), mean_wellbeing = mean(wellbeing), median_wellbeing = median(wellbeing))`} />

In Lesson 12-2 you saw Engineering's mean sit below its median: a tail of low
scores pulls the mean down. A rank test is not bothered by how far down that tail
reaches, only by where each employee falls in the overall order.

## Ranks, then an ordinary comparison

Rank all 480 wellbeing scores together, from 1 (lowest) to 480 (highest), then look
at the average rank in each department.

<CodeBlock id="c-ranks" code={`d <- d %>% mutate(wellbeing_rank = rank(wellbeing))

d %>%
  group_by(department) %>%
  summarise(mean_rank = mean(wellbeing_rank))`} />

If department made no difference, every department's mean rank would be near the
middle, about 240. Support sits far below the others. The Kruskal-Wallis statistic
*H* measures how far the mean ranks spread around that middle, and under the null
hypothesis it follows a chi-square distribution with *k* − 1 degrees of freedom,
where *k* is the number of groups.

<CodeBlock id="c-kw" code={`kruskal.test(wellbeing ~ department, data = d)`} />

R prints it as Kruskal-Wallis chi-squared, and the textbook reports it as
χ²(3, N = 480) = 25.55; *H*(3) = 25.55 is the same result.

The formula is the same one you would give `lm()`. What changes is the question:
not "do the means differ?" but "do the groups tend to sit at different places in
the order?"

## What it costs

A rank test answers a vaguer question. It tells you that the departments differ in
where their employees tend to fall, not by how many points of wellbeing, and it
does not come with coefficients, confidence intervals for differences, or a way to
add a covariate. That is why the linear model stays the default in this course,
and the rank test is the check you reach for when the assumptions of Lesson 11-4
clearly fail in a small sample.

To see **which** departments differ, follow up with pairwise Mann-Whitney tests,
adjusted for multiple comparisons just as in Lesson 12-3:

<CodeBlock id="c-pairwise" code={`pairwise.wilcox.test(d$wellbeing, d$department, p.adjust.method = "holm", exact = FALSE)`} />
